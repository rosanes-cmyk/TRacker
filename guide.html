01

Start here: pick your name

Ten seconds, once per device. Nothing works until you do it.

Open the link. At the top right you'll see a small box that says Logging as. Click it and choose yourself from the list.

Logging as Era ▾ Live
Top right of the tracker. The green Live dot means your work is saving for everyone.
  • Reps — pick your own first name: Era, Barbie, David or Diego.
  • Cherry and anyone in ops — pick Cherry / ops (view only), the last item on the list.

The tracker remembers your choice on that device, so you only do this once on your own phone or laptop. On a borrowed computer, check the name at the top before you start clicking.

Until you pick a name, everything is read-only — you can look, but the buttons won't do anything. That's on purpose, so nobody logs calls under the wrong name by accident.

02

Cherry's view vs. yours

Same link for everybody. The name you pick decides what appears.

This is the part people ask about most, so here it is plainly: there is only one link. Cherry does not get a different address, and neither does anyone else. The tracker simply shows you a different set of tabs depending on who you said you were.

If you picked a rep name Era, Barbie, David, Diego
  • You see Dashboard and your own tab — nothing else.
  • The other three reps' tabs are not on your screen at all.
  • You can log calls, texts, emails and notes on your own 100 leads.
  • Your CSV export gives you your 100 leads.
If you picked Cherry / ops Cherry and ops
  • You see Dashboard and all four rep tabs.
  • Every list is readable, and none of it is editable — logging is left to the reps.
  • You get the full rollup and can search all 400 leads.
  • Your CSV export gives you all 400 leads.
Dashboard Era 100 you
What Era sees — two tabs, and hers is marked.
Dashboard Era 100 Barbie 100 David 100 Diego 100
What Cherry sees — the whole floor, read-only.

One honest note. The tracker takes your word for who you are — there's no password. Anyone could pick a different name from that list. It's built to stop mistakes, not to stop someone who means it. So pick your own name, and if you ever need real logins, that's a conversation with Cherry.

Everyone sees the dashboard, including the rep-by-rep table. That's on purpose — it's the floor's scoreboard.

03

Logging a call

One click per dial. Never type a number.

Every lead on your board has three buttons under Log a call attempt. When you hang up, click the one that matches what happened:

Answered 2− VM left 0− No VM 0− VM full 1−
Dialed three times: picked up twice, and once the mailbox was full.
  • Answered — they picked up.
  • VM left — no answer, you left a voicemail.
  • No VM — no answer, and you chose not to leave one.
  • VM full — you tried to leave one but the mailbox was full. Different from No VM on purpose: you wanted to leave a message and couldn't. The box may be clear tomorrow, so keep the lead in play.

Each of those four clicks counts as one dial. You don't click anything extra — the Calls total next to them adds itself up. Call the same lead five times and the numbers just keep climbing.

Clicked the wrong one?

Every button has a small − on its right. One click takes that outcome back down by one, and removes the dial along with it. No harm done.

Why numbers and not a tick box

Because you call leads more than once. A tick box could only ever say "called at some point". The numbers tell Cherry you dialed this lead four times and got one pickup — which is the difference between a lead that's cold and one nobody has really worked.

06

Bad numbers and leads to stop calling

When a number will never reach the person, say so — don't keep dialing it.

Click the ✎ pencil on the lead's row. At the top of the box that opens is Lead status. Pick whichever fits:

WorkingWrong numberInvalid / disconnected Opted outDo not callNot the owner Out of buy box areaSkipped for now
Every lead starts on Working. Pick another and it leaves your call list.

The number is bad, the lead might still be good

  • Wrong number — a real person answered, just not the one you wanted.
  • Invalid / disconnected — not a working number at all.

These two go into a Needs new number pile. The owner may still be worth chasing — they just need a new number found. Filter to that pile and export it, and you have a skip-tracing list ready to hand off.

Stop contacting this lead

  • Opted out — they replied STOP, or asked you not to contact them.
  • Do not call — on a do-not-call list.
  • Not the owner — they don't own the property.

The lead is fine, the deal isn't

  • Out of buy box area — you reached them and it's a real lead, the property is just outside the area we buy in.

This one is different from the rest, and deliberately so: it still counts as a lead you contacted. You dialed, connected and qualified it — that's the job done, not a miss, so it stays in your contacted and response numbers. It only leaves your call list. It also sits in its own pile you can export, because an out-of-area lead is often worth something to an investor who does work that market.

Opted out and Do not call are not suggestions. Once one of those is set, that lead is off the list for good. Don't set it back to Working to have another go.

What it does to your numbers

A parked lead drops out of your list and out of every rate, so it stops counting against you. If 12 of your 100 turn out to be bad numbers, your board says 88 to work · 12 parked and your percentages are measured against the 88 you can actually reach. Cherry sees the parked count too, so nobody mistakes a dud number for a rep not calling.

Coming back to it later

  • Skipped for now — you're not calling this one yet. Not a wrong number, not a dead lead, just not now.

Skipping asks you why, and won't go through until you answer — one line is enough. "Asked me to call back Friday", "bad time, kids in the background", "needs a Spanish speaker". Type it and press Enter.

Skipped for now
The reason shows on the lead and how long it has sat there — "· Asked me to call back Friday · skipped 2d ago".

A skip is a promise to come back. It leaves your call list but it still counts as one of your leads, so skipping doesn't make your numbers look better — it just moves the lead. Cherry sees the count and how long each has been sitting. Use the Skipped chip to work through them.

Putting one back

Parked leads aren't lost — click the Needs new number, Closed, Out of buy box or Skipped chip above your list to see them. Each one carries a ↺ Put back on the call list button. One click and it's back where it was, with everything you'd logged on it still there.

↺ Put back on the call list
Only shows on a parked lead. You can also open the pencil and click Working.
07

Undoing a mistake

For everything the small − doesn't cover.

Above your list, on your own tab, there's an ↩ Undo button. It takes back the last thing you did — a call you logged, a Text or Email you ticked, a response you set, a lead you parked. Ctrl+Z (or ⌘Z) does the same. Keep clicking to walk further back.

↩ Undo
Hover it and it tells you exactly what it will take back — "Undo Do not call on Ellen Minor".
  • It only ever undoes your own changes, from this sitting. Close the tab and the list starts fresh.
  • If someone else has changed that lead since you did, it stops and says so rather than wiping their work. Open the lead and sort it out by hand.
  • Notes aren't in the list — the text is right there in the box, so just edit it.
08

Notes on a lead

Paste a whole call summary if you want to. There's no length limit.

  1. Click the small ✎ pencil at the right end of the lead's row. The box has the lead status at the top and the note underneath.
  2. Type or paste. It saves as you go — there's no save button.
  3. Click the pencil again to put it away.

A pencil with a teal box around it means that lead already has a note. Hover it and you'll see the first line without opening anything.

Got a lot of notes and want your list back? The Show notes button above the list opens every note at once, and turns into Hide notes to close them all. The number next to it tells you how many leads on screen have one.

09

Finding one lead fast

There are two searches, and they do different jobs.

  • On your own board — the box that says Find a lead in this list. Searches your 100 by name, phone number or REI contact ID.
  • On the dashboard — Find a lead searches all 400 across every rep. Useful before you dial a number you half-recognize: if it's on someone else's list, it tells you whose.

You can also narrow your board without typing. The round buttons — Not called yet, Called, Answered, Responded, HOT, Distress — filter it instantly. Not called yet first thing in the morning is the fastest way to find your next dial.

10

Reading the dashboard

Everybody can see it. Here's what each part is telling you.

The big percentage
How much of the 400-lead file has any activity logged against it at all.
The colored bar
All 400 leads split into four groups — not called yet, texted or emailed, called, responded. Light means further along. You want the light end growing.
Response by channel
Of the leads you called, how many picked up. Of the leads you texted, how many wrote back. Same for email. Each one is measured only against the leads that channel was actually used on, so you can tell which method is worth the time.
Parked
Leads taken off the call list — bad numbers, opt-outs, wrong owner. They show as their own slice on the bar and come out of every rate, so a file full of dud numbers doesn't read as reps not working.
Out of buy box
Leads you reached that fall outside the area we buy in. Counted apart from bad numbers, because the rep did the work — these still count as contacted.
Skipped
Leads a rep set aside with a reason, meaning to come back. Counted apart from the dead ones — this is work deferred, not work finished, and it still counts as that rep's lead.
Rep by rep · Coverage
The share of that rep's 100 with any activity logged. This is the "is everyone working their list" number.
Last activity
When that rep last logged real work. Green is recent, amber is getting quiet, red means nothing in over a day. It counts logged work only — opening the page doesn't reset it.
Show every column
Swaps the summary table for the full one: calls, answered, voicemails, texts, emails and every response rate, rep by rep.

Everything is live. When a rep logs a call, these numbers move within seconds — no refresh, no waiting for a report.

Getting the data out

For a spreadsheet, a meeting, or your own records.

Export Era (CSV)Export all (CSV)Export rollup
The button names itself after whatever you're allowed to export.
  • The Export button at the top right saves a spreadsheet file — every lead with its name, phone, REI link, all the counts, notes and who last touched it.
  • A rep gets their own 100. Cherry gets all 400.
  • Filter first and the export follows. Click Needs new number and the button changes to Export these 12 — that's your skip-tracing hand-off, ready to send.
  • Export rollup at the bottom of the dashboard saves the summary instead — one row per rep, with every rate.

The file opens in Excel, Numbers or Google Sheets. Your browser will ask you to confirm the download first.

12

If something looks wrong

My clicks aren't doing anything

Check the name at the top right. If it says Choose…, pick yourself first. If it says another rep's name, switch to yours — you can only edit your own leads.

It says "On this device only" in red

The tracker has lost touch with the shared database, and your changes are only being kept in that browser. Reload the page. If it stays red, tell Cherry before you carry on logging — otherwise the work won't reach anyone else.

On this device only
Another rep's tab has disappeared

That's how it's meant to be. Reps only see their own list. If you're Cherry and need all four, pick Cherry / ops (view only) from the name box.

A rep shows "never" under Last activity but they've been on the page

That column counts logged work — a call, a text, an email, a response or a note. Opening the tracker, or clicking something and undoing it, doesn't count. "Never" means nothing has actually been logged yet.

I don't want to call this one right now

Open the pencil and pick Skipped for now. It asks why — answer it and press Enter. The lead leaves your list and waits under the Skipped chip until you come back to it. Use this instead of just scrolling past, so nothing gets quietly forgotten.

The voicemail was full — which button?

VM full, the fourth one. Use No VM only when you chose not to leave a message.

It's a wrong number — do I just delete it?

No, and please don't leave it sitting in the list either. Open the pencil and set Wrong number. It leaves your call list, stops counting against your numbers, and lands in a pile that can be sent for skip tracing to find a good number.

I logged a call by mistake

Click the small − next to whichever outcome you hit. It removes the outcome and the dial together. For anything else — a wrong tick, a wrong response, a lead parked by accident — use ↩ Undo above your list, or Ctrl+Z.

Undo says someone else changed the lead

Exactly what it sounds like: another person edited that lead after you did, so undoing would wipe their work. Nothing was changed. Open the lead, see what it says now, and fix it by hand.

I pasted a lead and got a pile of junk rows

That happens when you copy a lead's row off the screen and paste it in — the tracker sees the name, the HOT tag, the phone and the REI link as four separate leads. The Add box now checks your list first and shows you what it will create, so a pasted row gets caught before anything is added. Only ever paste names, one per line.

Can I use this on my phone?

Yes. Open the same link in your phone's browser and the rows stack into cards sized for your thumb. Pick your name once and it remembers. Tapping a lead's phone number starts the call.
